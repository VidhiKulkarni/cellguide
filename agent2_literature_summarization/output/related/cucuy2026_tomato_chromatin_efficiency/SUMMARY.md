# Agent 2 structured extraction — cucuy2026_tomato_chromatin_efficiency

1. **Experimental design**: KO (Cas9 nuclease cleavage / indel formation), delivered as pre-assembled Cas9 ribonucleoprotein (RNP) by PEG-mediated transformation of tomato protoplasts; editing efficiency and repair footprints read out at 48 h by PCR amplicon sequencing. Matched ATAC-seq (chromatin accessibility, called open vs closed per target site) and MARS-seq (transcriptional state: low/medium/high) were generated in the same protoplast system. 94 of 420 sgRNAs were run in 2–5 biological replicates and 84 in a normalization batch to correct batch effects. Azimuth (Doench et al. 2016) was applied computationally as a cross-species prediction benchmark. Methods section body was not present in the saved Paperclip text.
2. **Target genes**: 420 unique sgRNAs targeting the promoter, first exon and first intron of 137 tomato genes (breeding genes from Rothan et al. 2019 plus chromatin modifiers, DNA-repair and housekeeping genes), distributed across all 12 chromosomes (~34 targets/chromosome, mostly subtelomeric); most genes have 3 sgRNAs (range 1–7). Individual gene symbols are listed only in Supplementary Data 1, which is not in the saved text.
3. **Outcomes**: On-target — editing efficiency across the 420 guides was trimodal, 0% to ~100%, median 34.5%. Open chromatin gave significantly higher editing than closed (P < 0.005, Mann-Whitney); genomic feature was only marginally significant overall (P = 0.52, Kruskal-Wallis) with promoter > exon (P = 0.048, Dunn's); transcriptional state had no detectable effect on efficiency. Within-gene SD of efficiency (24.05) was significantly below the global SD (27.22; permutation P = 0.001), indicating a local genomic-context effect, and guides in different features of the same gene correlated modestly (r ~ 0.25, P < 0.05) vs r ~ 0 shuffled. Repair: deletions were 81.6% of mutations, +1 insertions 17.1%; low-efficiency guides were dominated by -1 deletions while high-efficiency guides showed longer deletions (P < 0.001), near-absent insertions, enriched microhomology-associated deletions (P = 0.015) and longer MH tracts (P = 4.69e-5), with A/T enrichment adjacent to the cut site — an MMEJ signature mirrored in the human Leenay primary-T-cell dataset (1,656 sites). Deletions were significantly longer in closed than open chromatin (P < 0.001). Cross-species prediction: Azimuth score vs measured efficiency gave r = 0.05 (P > 0.05) in tomato, r = 0.17 in the human Leenay set and r = 0.13 in the CRISPRon set (10,592 SpCas9 sgRNAs); Azimuth bins separated efficiency significantly in human data (Kruskal-Wallis P < 0.001) but not in tomato. Off-target — not addressed/not reported; the terms off-target and specificity do not appear in the paper.
4. **Data type**: Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum cv. M82) mesophyll-derived protoplasts, non-dividing and arrested in G1 — a single uniform cellular system; new M82 protoplast ATAC-seq and MARS-seq datasets were generated. Human comparison datasets are re-analyzed public data: Leenay et al. 2019 (primary human T cells, 1,656 sites) and CRISPRon/Xiang et al. 2021 (human cells, 10,592 sgRNAs).

**Relevance flag**: Highly relevant to the efficiency term and to the cross-context transferability question: it is an ATAC-matched, 420-guide efficiency dataset in one uniform cell system showing open chromatin significantly raises Cas9 editing while transcription does not, i.e. accessibility rather than expression is the usable feature. It also provides a direct negative control on sequence-only scoring — human-trained Azimuth fails to rank guides in tomato (r = 0.05) and is weak even within human data (r = 0.13–0.17) — and explicitly cites Ito et al. 2024 as support for epigenome-augmented efficiency prediction. Contributes nothing to a specificity/off-target term, and the plant system limits direct numeric transfer to human T cells.
