# Agent 2 structured extraction — feng2024_crispri_hpsc_map

1. **Experimental design**: Genome-scale CRISPRi knockdown with single-cell RNA-seq readout (Perturb-seq). dCas9-KRAB-MeCP2 was introduced by piggyBac transposition (pB-CAGGS-dCas9-KRAB-MeCP2-BSD-mScarlet, derived from Addgene 110824) with blasticidin selection and per-line activity validation using a GFP-TSS-targeting reporter; the gRNA library was cloned into the lentiviral vector pKLV2-U6gRNA5(BbsI)-ccdb-PGKpuroBFP-W (Addgene 67974) and transduced at MOI ~0.2. Cells were sorted on viability/BFP/mScarlet and harvested 3-6 days post-infection (a 14-day arm was also tested), then profiled with Chromium Next GEM Single Cell 5-prime Kit v2 using a modified GEM mix with an extra primer for direct gRNA capture. Two designs: a genome-scale screen and a targeted 444-gene multi-donor screen.
2. **Target genes**: Genome-scale arm — over 20,000 gRNAs (3 gRNAs/gene from the Dolcetto library plus 40 non-targeting) against 7,226 genes, of which 6,673 (92%) passed the 10-cell coverage threshold; targets were selected as genes with iPSC growth defects (2,264), cancer-cell-line growth defects (4,594; 1,725 overlapping), or high iPSC expression (2,093). Targeted multi-donor arm — 1,355 guides against 444 genes plus 20 non-targeting guides. Further experiments used 483 guides against 161 genes. Genes named in results: PAF1, CTR9, RTF1, CDC73, CNOT1, CNOT3, MAX, OCT4, SNRNP70, SRSF1, ZMAT2, FA2H, CACNA1A, EIF3B, EIF3C, BACH1 (repressor; knockdown up-regulated HMOX1), CREB3L2 to MICU2, ZNF208 to SEH1L, ZNF611 to MOB1A, PRKD3, C9orf135, MRPL55, MT-CYB.
3. **Outcomes**: On-target — target-gene down-regulation and downstream trans effects. Nearly 2 million cells total; 219,206 cells assigned to a source line and targeting guide in the genome-scale arm (median 8 cells/gRNA, 25 cells/gene) plus 499,998 control cells; 2,900 of 4,874 (60%) knockdowns of an expressed gene gave significant down-regulation (median log-fold change -0.42). Only 0.02% of target-gene pairs showed trans down-regulation (8,833 of 43,176,109 tested); 51% of targets (3,374/6,674) had at least one significant trans effect, mean 2.57 per target; PAF1/CTR9/RTF1 had 962/847/594. Targeted arm: 635,022 of 1,161,864 cells assigned across 19 lines, over 90% of target genes significantly down-regulated in 15 of 19 lines, trans effects replicating the genome-scale screen at Pearson R = 0.73. All 17 significant trans effects overlapping annotated TF-target relationships matched the expected direction (p = 0.02, binomial). Cross-context/cross-background result: guide identity was the single largest source of variation in observed trans effects, with different guides against the same target only moderately consistent (median Pearson R = 0.61); CRISPRi efficacy varied strongly by cell line — 4 of 19 lines failed to significantly change over 50% of the library and showed muted global transcriptional response — and both on-target efficacy and gRNA assignment rate were highly predicted by dCas9 expression level in the line. Timing mattered: at 14 days post-infection only 34% of cells were guide-assigned with no target significantly down-regulated, versus 52% assignment and 59% of targets significantly changed for weak-growth-effect genes. Of 68,321 line-specific trans effects tested, 2,941 (4.3%) were heritable (donor component explained a significant variance fraction). Off-target — explicitly quantified. Of 2,067 potential off-target trans effects (guides with sequence similarity elsewhere in the genome, particularly near another gene TSS), 112 showed significant repression of the predicted off-target gene, and these exhibited on average 24-fold greater guide-driven variance than other trans effects (p = 1.3e-05, two-tailed t-test). The authors additionally flagged any guide whose first 9 nt of seed sequence mapped within 2 kb of an OCT4 TSS (FANTOM5) and replicated seed-mediated off-target repression of OCT4 for those guides; they caution that even stricter criteria might be needed to rule out all possible off-target effects. Off-target and cross-mapping effects were filtered out before the heritability analysis.
4. **Data type**: Human induced pluripotent stem cells — 34 high-quality iPSC lines from 26 healthy donors (genome-scale arm), 20 lines from 10 donors with 2 lines per donor (targeted arm, 19 analyzed), and a separate 24-line pooling experiment; cultured on Vitronectin XF in mTeSR Plus. HEK293FT was used only for lentivirus packaging. No animal model or tissue.

**Relevance flag**: Highly relevant, and the strongest cross-context transferability evidence in this batch. It quantifies exactly the problem CellGuide targets: the same guide knockdown efficacy and downstream signature vary substantially across 34 human genetic backgrounds, with cell-line and effector-dose effects large enough that 4 of 19 lines were effectively non-responsive, establishing that a sequence-only guide score cannot be context-invariant. It also supplies a directly usable specificity-term precedent: a seed-sequence off-target model (9 nt seed complementarity within 2 kb of another gene TSS) with an empirical hit rate of 112 of 2,067 predicted off-target trans effects and a measurable 24-fold inflation of guide-driven variance — a concrete template for an off-target penalty. Caveat: this is CRISPRi (dCas9-KRAB-MeCP2, promoter-proximal binding) in iPSCs rather than SpCas9 nuclease indel formation in T cells, so the efficiency mechanism differs, and no ATAC or chromatin-accessibility feature is reported directly.
