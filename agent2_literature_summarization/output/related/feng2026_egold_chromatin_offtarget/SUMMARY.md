# Agent 2 structured extraction — feng2026_egold_chromatin_offtarget

1. **Experimental design**: KO (nuclease) and base-editing off-target profiling plus computational ML modeling. EGOLD (Endogenous Genome-wide Off-target Library Detection) co-transfects (PEI, plasmid) one of 17 editors — WT Cas9, eCas9, HiFiCas9, HypaCas9, SuperFiCas9, OptiCas9, Sniper2LCas9, RYCas9, and nCas9/nRYCas9/nSuperFiCas9 fusions with TadA-8e, rAPOBEC1 or YE1 — with a single sgRNA; EGFP+/mCherry+ cells are FACS-sorted at 48 h and edits called by ~40x WGS (BWA-MEM + GATK4 Mutect2) across thousands of endogenous, sequence-identical repeat-derived candidate off-target sites. Chromatin features are taken from ENCODE bedgraphs and a random-forest off-target predictor is trained on the result.
2. **Target genes**: No endogenous disease genes — the design deliberately targets repetitive/sequence-redundant loci to obtain many sequence-identical off-target sites in differing chromatin contexts. 223,349,907 candidate sites were filtered to 24,116 viable candidate protospacers, then the top 3,000 by callable off-target count; 10 were tested and "site 1" (5'-ACTCCAGCCTGGGTGACAAG + AGG-3') was the primary target, with sites 2, 5, 6, 7 and 10 for replication. Sequence-identical off-target sites were grouped into "types" (e.g. Type 2 = 2,000 identical sites genome-wide) to hold sequence constant.
3. **Outcomes**: On-target — on-target efficiency was measured mainly to qualify editors (SuperFiCas9 retained >25% on-target efficiency while producing the fewest off-targets; RYCas9 produced the most off-targets); on-target-vs-chromatin was not the paper's focus. Off-target — 2,145,592 total off-targets across 17 tools (1,236–618,774 per tool); Cas9-variant off-target InDels ranged 11–2,799 per editor and base-editor off-target SNVs 478 ± 332 to 23,854 ± 2,001. 40–80% of the potential off-target pool was chromatin-influenced (base editors: 46.67%–82.35% of off-target types significantly associated with at least one of 13 chromatin features, Wilcoxon P < 0.05). With sequence held constant, edited off-target sites were significantly enriched in open chromatin relative to unedited sites; DNA methylation and H3K9me3 were negatively correlated with off-target editing, while accessibility-linked features (ATAC-seq, DNase-seq, RNAP-II, ELK4, TCF7L2, TRIM28, etc.) were positively correlated. The chromatin dependence increased with sgRNA–DNA mismatch count — perfectly matched and 1-mismatch edits were largely chromatin-independent. Off-target site sets overlapped poorly across editors (78–95% unique sites between a nuclease and its derived base editors) and between HEK293T and K562 cells. Random-forest models trained on 75 features (62 sequence-derived + 13 chromatin/epigenetic) reached 97.87%–99.97% test accuracy; mismatch count was the single strongest feature (importance 0.023–0.347), while chromatin features were individually modest but collectively contributed cumulative importance 0.039–0.193.
4. **Data type**: Human HEK293T cells (DMEM + 10% FBS) for all primary EGOLD experiments and model training; human K562 cells used for a cross-cell-line off-target overlap comparison. Chromatin/epigenetic tracks from ENCODE; hg38 reference. Toolkit/models: https://github.com/offtargetor/EGOLD.

**Relevance flag**: Directly usable for the specificity term — the strongest available evidence that off-target risk is a cell-context property, not just a spacer property: with sequence held identical, off-target editing is significantly more frequent in open chromatin, suppressed by DNA methylation and H3K9me3, and increasingly chromatin-dependent as mismatch count rises (so an accessibility term should raise both the efficiency and the off-target-risk estimate for a guide). It supplies a concrete feature recipe (13 ENCODE chromatin/epigenetic tracks averaged over a 50 bp window = 23 bp protospacer + 15 bp upstream + 12 bp downstream) and a quantified weighting split (mismatch count dominant; chromatin features 0.039–0.193 cumulative importance) for a chromatin-aware specificity score. The HEK293T-vs-K562 off-target non-overlap is a cross-cell-type transferability signal. Caveat: data derive from repetitive loci, which the authors note may not transfer cleanly to single-copy protein-coding targets.
