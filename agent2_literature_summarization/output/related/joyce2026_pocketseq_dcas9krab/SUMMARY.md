# Agent 2 structured extraction — joyce2026_pocketseq_dcas9krab

1. **Experimental design**: CRISPRi (no cutting). A genome-wide pooled dCas9-KRAB CRISPRi screen (Weissman/Horlbeck library, lentiviral BFP/puro gRNA vectors, spinfection at <20% infection to enforce single integration, puromycin selection) in cells with stably integrated HA-tagged dCas9-KRAB, followed by the new method POCKET-seq (Profiling Off-target CRISPR Knockdown through Epitope Tag): anti-HA ChIP-seq of dCas9-KRAB (Abcam ab9110, NovaSeq PE150), Bowtie2 alignment to hg19, MACS3 paired-end peak calling, then a custom Python annotator that takes each peak midpoint, finds the nearest TSS strand-aware in a GFF3, flags peaks within 1 kb, and ranks binding events by ChIP signal, GO-term membership, or primary-screen epsilon phenotype score. Secondary validation used mVenus-PTS / PMP70 fluorescence and immunofluorescence microscopy (CellProfiler), RT-qPCR, and cDNA add-back rescue.
2. **Target genes**: Genome-wide CRISPRi library in the primary screen (peroxisome-import phenotype via a sequestered mVenus-ZeoR-PTS zeocin-resistance reporter). Deep characterization focused on *SOCS3* (guides SOCS3_g1 and SOCS3_g4) and the off-target gene *PEX19*; benchmarking also used two published guides, VEGFA site 2 and HEK293 site 4. Rescue constructs: *PEX19* cDNA (pCR2110) and *SOCS3* cDNA (pCR2111).
3. **Outcomes**: On-target — both SOCS3 guides loaded dCas9-KRAB onto the *SOCS3* promoter as intended and both depleted *SOCS3* by RT-qPCR, yet only SOCS3_g1 reduced peroxisome abundance; *SOCS3* cDNA re-expression failed to rescue that phenotype, showing the phenotype was not on-target. Off-target — this is the paper's core result and is quantified. POCKET-seq found 334 off-target dCas9-KRAB binding sites for SOCS3_g1 vs 47 for SOCS3_g4 (n = 2 biological replicates); only SOCS3_g1 had an off-target in the "peroxisome organization" GO term, namely the *PEX19* promoter, and RT-qPCR confirmed SOCS3_g1 (not g4) depleted *PEX19*, with *PEX19* cDNA add-back rescuing the phenotype. In silico prediction badly missed these: CRISPOR predicted 191 sites for SOCS3_g1, of which exactly 1 overlapped the 334 measured sites and it was not *PEX19*; for SOCS3_g4, CRISPOR's 52 predicted sites had zero overlap with the 47 measured. Cross-method comparisons for VEGFA_2: CRISPOR 499 predicted, GUIDE-seq 152 (U2OS), DISCOVER-seq+ 184 (HEK293T), POCKET-seq 40 (HCT116); for HEK293_s4: CRISPOR 895, GUIDE-seq 134, HA-dCas9 484 (HEK293T), POCKET-seq 3,429 (HCT116) — all with little overlap. The authors attribute the mismatch to CRISPOR relying on sequence mismatch rules and being "unable to account for the local chromatin context of potential binding events," and to cleavage-based assays (GUIDE-seq, DISCOVER-seq+) structurally missing binding-only events; they also invoke cell-line-specific chromatin effects and sustained (integrated) vs transient dCas9 expression as drivers of the difference.
4. **Data type**: Human cell lines — HCT116 colorectal carcinoma stably expressing HA-dCas9-KRAB (gift of the Corn lab, ETH Zürich) for all screening, ChIP-seq, microscopy, and RT-qPCR; HEK293T used only for lentiviral packaging. Comparison datasets are from published U2OS (GUIDE-seq) and HEK293/HEK293T (GUIDE-seq, DISCOVER-seq+, HA-dCas9) experiments. No primary cells, animal model, or tissue.

**Relevance flag**: Highly relevant to the specificity half of the metric — it is direct experimental evidence that a sequence-mismatch off-target predictor (CRISPOR) has near-zero overlap (1/334 and 0/47) with measured dCas9 binding off-targets, explicitly because it ignores local chromatin context, which argues that our specificity term should be chromatin-weighted rather than mismatch-only. It also supplies concrete per-guide off-target counts (SOCS3_g1 334 vs SOCS3_g4 47; VEGFA_2 40; HEK293_s4 3,429) and a cell-type-dependence signal (off-target counts differ across HCT116 / U2OS / HEK293T), plus a functionally meaningful failure mode: an off-target that lands within 1 kb of a phenotype-relevant promoter. Caveat: it measures dCas9 *binding*, not SpCas9 cleavage, so the counts are an upper bound relative to nuclease off-target editing, and it reports no ATAC/accessibility track of its own.
