# Agent 2 structured extraction — kiattisewee2025_bacterial_crispra_rules

1. **Experimental design**: CRISPRa in bacteria (*E. coli*), plasmid-encoded and delivered by transformation — catalytically dead Cas proteins (Spy-dCas9, Sth1-dCas9, Lb-dCas12a) with activator effectors recruited either as direct dCas9 fusions (dCas9-AsiA) or non-covalently via engineered MS2/PP7/boxB scaffold RNAs (scRNAs) with MCP/PCP/λN22 fusions, plus SYNZIP protein-protein recruitment. Readout is a bulk plate-reader fluorescence assay (mRFP1 / OD600) on synthetic reporter promoters, with sgRNA target sites tiled at 10 bp and single-base resolution upstream of the TSS.
2. **Target genes**: No endogenous genes. Targets are sgRNA/scRNA sites in synthetic mRFP reporter promoters: the J1 PAM-tiling array (NGG PAMs every 10 bp on both strands) upstream of the BBa_J23117 σ70 minimal promoter, plus hybrids J1-J3(100), J1-J2(100)-J3(100), J1-J3(122); σ54 promoters J1-pspAp and pspAp-G6; the σ38 sodCp promoter; and the published W1 reporter. Effector proteins tested (not targets): SoxS (R93A/S101A), MarA, TetD, αNTD (RpoA N-terminal domain), Crl, AsiA (AsiA_m2.1), PspF. hAAVS1 was the non-targeting/off-target control scRNA.
3. **Outcomes**: On-target — fold-activation of mRFP vs. sgRNA distance/strand/phase relative to the TSS. Optimal target windows differ by up to ~200 bp between effectors: SoxS optimal at −81 (non-template), αNTD at −70 (template), both within a 60–100 bp upstream window conserved across σ70, σ38, σ32, σ24 promoters; PspF optimal at −60/−61 non-template on σ54 promoters (8-fold in J1-pspAp, ~16-fold in pspAp-G6); AsiA optimal far upstream at −192/−202 (~30-fold in J1-J3(122)) yet inactive at −182 bp, the site previously reported optimal in the W1 reporter — i.e. precise optimal positions are promoter-context-dependent for the same effector, with sharp single-base phase dependence. Best single effector was MCP-SoxS at 27-fold (20–40-fold across constructs). Effector combinations were antagonistic or neutral: MCP-SoxS-TetD 7-fold vs 27-fold for MCP-SoxS alone; αNTD-MCP-SoxS 1.3-fold vs 3.3-fold (αNTD-MCP) and 27-fold (MCP-SoxS); orthogonal Spy-dCas9-AsiA + Sth1-dCas9/MCP-SoxS 12-fold vs 30-fold (AsiA alone) and 14-fold (SoxS alone). The only apparent synergy was PspF(−60) + SoxS(−100) at 18-fold, a 1.8-fold gain over PspF alone, of which only ~1.1-fold is attributable to SoxS itself (1.2-fold from the second dCas9 complex, 1.3-fold from the MS2 hairpin). Off-target — not addressed as a genome-wide or mismatch-tolerance phenomenon. "Off-target" in this paper means only negative-control guides (hAAVS1 scRNA, "OT" sites) used as activation baselines. Related observations: native DNA-binding motifs of the AraC/XylS effectors were mutated to prevent sequestration at off-target endogenous sequences (effective for SoxS previously, but MarA and TetD mutants gave no significant improvement), and MCP-Crl raised reporter expression 2.4-fold with no CRISPR recruitment at all, i.e. non-specific effector activity.
4. **Data type**: Bacterial only — *E. coli* (cloning in DH10B / NEB Turbo; reporter assays in *E. coli* cultured in EZ-RDM). No mammalian cells, primary cells, animal model, or tissue.

**Relevance flag**: Only tangentially relevant to CellGuide. It contains no chromatin/ATAC/epigenetic data (bacteria lack chromatin), no mammalian cell types, and no off-target quantification usable as a specificity term. Its one transferable idea for the efficiency term is a strong demonstration that guide activity is governed by local promoter/regulatory context rather than spacer sequence alone — the same effector's optimal distance-to-TSS shifted between promoters and activity varied sharply with single-base phase — a bacterial analogue of the cell-context dependence CellGuide models, but the mechanism (direct RNAP contact geometry) does not transfer to SpCas9 nuclease editing in human cells.
