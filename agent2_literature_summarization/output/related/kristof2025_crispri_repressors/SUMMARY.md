# Agent 2 structured extraction — kristof2025_crispri_repressors

1. **Experimental design**: **CRISPRi effector-protein engineering** — not a guide-design study. Over 100 bipartite and tripartite dCas9-repressor fusion proteins were built by Golden Gate combinatorial assembly (42-variant bipartite library screened as 192 single replicates for 99% coverage; 72-variant tripartite library as 288 replicates for 98% coverage) and screened for knockdown. Delivery: **transient co-transfection** for reporter/RT-qPCR assays in HEK293T and **lentiviral transduction for stable dCas9-repressor cell lines** in A549 and HCT116. Both **direct dCas9 fusion and scaffold-based recruitment (PP7 stem loops on the sgRNA + PCP-repressor)** modalities were tested. Assays: SV40-promoter-eGFP flow-cytometry reporter (mCherry-gated for effector expression), RT-qPCR of endogenous transcripts, surface-marker flow cytometry, competitive growth/dropout assays, and a **genome-wide pooled CRISPRi dropout screen (Dolcetto Set A, 21-day outgrowth, MAGeCK)**.
2. **Target genes**: Reporter — SV40-eGFP cassette (9-sgRNA tiling panel across template and non-template strands, up- and downstream of the eGFP TSS). Endogenous coding genes — **CANX, BLM, SEL1L, SYVN1** (knockdown panel across four cell lines) and essential genes **DNAJC19** (highly essential), **KRAS** (moderately essential), **MRPS11** (marginally essential), 3 sgRNAs each. lncRNAs — **MINCR, DANCR**. Surface markers — **CD81, CD151** (using previously characterized "strong" and "weak" sgRNA sequences for each). Off-target/spreading panel — SYVN1's five nearest neighbors **ZNHIT2, FAU, MRPL49, SPDYC, CAPN1**. Genome-wide screen — **18,901 genes** (Dolcetto Set A), scored against 215 core-essential and 542 non-essential reference genes. Repressor domains screened include KRBOX1(KRAB), KOX1(KRAB), ZIM3(KRAB), MeCP2 and a truncated 80-aa MeCP2(t), SCMH1, CTCF, RCOR1, IKZF5, MAX, IRF2BP1.
3. **Outcomes**: On-target — the best bipartite fusions improved eGFP knockdown ~20-30% over dCas9-ZIM3(KRAB); the top tripartite dCas9-ZIM3(KRAB)-MAX-MeCP2(t) added a further ~10%. Across 6 cell lines, **dCas9-ZIM3(KRAB)-MeCP2(t) and dCas9-KOX1(KRAB)-MeCP2(t) outperformed dCas9-ZIM3(KRAB) by an average of 50% and 31%** respectively. On endogenous loci, ZIM3(KRAB)-MAX-MeCP2(t) both raised and **tightened** knockdown (65-80% across BLM/CANX/SEL1L/SYVN1 vs a wide **40-75% for ZIM3(KRAB)** alone), and improved 8 of 9 guides in the tiling panel regardless of TSS position or strand. Genome-wide, dCas9-ZIM3(KRAB)-MeCP2(t) recovered **1,719 essential genes vs 696 for dCas9-ZIM3(KRAB) and 636 for dCas9-KOX1(KRAB)-MeCP2**, close to the ~1,800 reported for Cas9 nuclease screens in HCT116, with strongest core-essential depletion and no depletion of core non-essential controls. Off-target — addressed as **local transcriptional spreading and non-specific effector toxicity, not as sequence-mismatch off-targeting**. Targeting SYVN1 in HEK293T, dCas9-ZIM3(KRAB)-MeCP2(t) gave the highest on-target knockdown **without significantly altering any of the five nearest-neighbor genes** (ZNHIT2, FAU, MRPL49, SPDYC, CAPN1). Long-term stable expression of most repressor fusions was non-toxic in A549, though dCas9-KOX1(KRAB) (~1.7%/day) and SID4x-dCas9-KOX1(KRAB) (~1%/day) grew slightly faster than eGFP controls — contradicting a prior K562 report of ~6%/day proliferation loss for SID4x. No mismatch/genome-wide off-target binding analysis (no ChIP-seq, GUIDE-seq, or Cas-OFFinder) is reported.
4. **Data type**: Immortalized cell lines only — **HEK293T** (primary screening host), **A549** (human lung adenocarcinoma; essential-gene and toxicity work), **HCT116** (human colon carcinoma; genome-wide screen), **HeLa** (human cervical carcinoma), plus **CHO-K1** (Chinese hamster ovary), **Neuro2A** (mouse neuroblast), and **NIH-3T3** (mouse embryonic fibroblast) in the cross-species/cross-line effector comparison. K562 appears only as a literature comparison. No primary cells, no animal model, no tissue.

**Relevance flag**: Relevant mainly as evidence for *why* a cell-context term is needed, and as a caution about confounders — it is not itself a chromatin-feature or off-target-scoring resource. Two findings are directly usable. First, **guide-sequence-dependent variance is real and effector-dependent**: the 9-guide TSS-tiling panel and the "strong" vs "weak" CD81/CD151 guide pairs show that the spread between good and bad guides shrinks with a stronger repressor (40-75% -> 65-80% knockdown range), meaning any efficiency score fitted on one CRISPRi effector will not transfer cleanly to another and the effector must be a covariate in the metric. Second, it gives an unusually explicit **cell-context dependence with a mechanistic explanation**: MAX-containing fusions work well in HEK293T but fail in A549/HCT116/HeLa, attributed to cell-line-specific abundance of MAX-dimerizing cofactors (high MYC in HeLa; low MXD/MNT repressive cofactors relative to MYC in A549/HCT116) — a trans-factor-availability form of cell context distinct from, but complementary to, chromatin accessibility. The authors also attribute residual per-guide knockdown variation to "individual sgRNA binding dynamics or local chromatin architecture", though they do not measure it. For a specificity term the SYVN1 five-nearest-neighbor assay is a usable but narrow proxy for CRISPRi spreading/local off-target; there is no mismatch-based off-target data here.
