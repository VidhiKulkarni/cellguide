# Agent 2 structured extraction — moore2025_truncated_crispri

1. **Experimental design**: **CRISPRi** (with a head-to-head Cas9 nuclease KO comparison), delivered by an **all-in-one KRAB-dCas9-puro lentiviral vector (pXPR_066, Broad GPP)** at MOI ~0.35-0.70 with puromycin selection. The central manipulation is **guide spacer length**: successive 5' truncations of a 20 nt spacer down to 8 nt, so that a single short guide matches hundreds of genomic sites simultaneously ("multi-locus CRISPRi"). Assays: flow cytometry of a surface-protein reporter (CD81-FITC), RT-qPCR of enhancer-target gene expression, a 21-day pooled lentiviral dropout/enrichment fitness screen scored as a z-score against 15 full-length safe-harbor guides, **CTCF ChIP-seq, H3K9me3 ChIP-seq, and dCas9 ChIP-seq**, and RNA-seq (dream differential expression).
2. **Target genes**: **CD81** (promoter/TSS CRISPRi reporter, sgCD81i-1/-2/-3; plus exon-1 KO guides sgCD81-KO-1/-2), **EPB41** (via 4 TF motifs — PU.1, SP1, YY1, NR2 — in a 570 bp enhancer at chr1:28,883,749-28,884,318, ~2.8 kb upstream), and **CTCF** — both the CTCF gene itself (full-length promoter guides as a positive control, selected with CRISPick) and, as the main target class, **13,352 genome-wide CTCF motif sites** hit by a library of 24 10 nt guides (plus 96 11 nt derivatives). Not endogenous-gene-centric: the screen's unit is a TF binding site, not a gene. Sites per guide ranged from 182 (sg1) to 1,123 (sg24); sg4 = 357 perfect-match sites, sg8 = 465. The paper also nominates **88 additional vertebrate TFs** whose JASPAR motifs contain a well-positioned NGG PAM.
3. **Outcomes**: On-target — CRISPRi repression of CD81 persisted down to a **9 nt target match** (g[9nt] active, g[8nt] complete loss), whereas **Cas9 cleavage failed below a 17 nt match** — the guide-length requirements for binding vs cleavage diverge below 17 nt. Truncated guides worked in both Jurkat and A375, and 3 of 4 11 nt motif-directed guides repressed EPB41 as well as the respective 20 nt guide (one-way ANOVA P < 0.0001 vs safe harbor across all lengths). In the pooled screen a single 10 nt guide (sg4) produced **significant CTCF displacement at 64% of its perfect-match bound sites (79/123, P < 1e-5, LFC < 0.5)** with concurrent significant H3K9me3 gain (t-test P = 1.3e-38) and strong dCas9 occupancy; sg8 lost CTCF at **55.7% (229/411)** of its bound sites. A 13 nt version (aag[sg4], 77 match sites) gave a stronger effect at shared sites (mean CTCF binding ratio 0.34 vs safe, versus 0.54 for sg4). Off-target — quantified directly, and this is a key contribution. Of **29,908 "partial match" sites (1-5 mismatches to sg4), only 78 showed significant CTCF loss**, i.e. "most partial match sites remained unaffected... indicating strong sequence specificity"; most affected partial matches carried only a 1 nt mismatch, tolerated at A/T-rich and 5'-distal positions, while **PAM-proximal (3' seed) mismatches were not tolerated**. Transcriptome off-target: RNA-seq in A375 showed the g[9nt] CD81 guide caused **34 significantly upregulated and ~32 additional downregulated genes vs only 2 upregulated for the 20 nt guide**. In Jurkat, sg4 produced no differentially expressed genes while sg8 upregulated 67 genes (keratin/intermediate-filament programs). Adding 1-2 unpaired 5' bases retained activity; 3 unpaired bases (gcc) abrogated repression entirely, and 11 nt-vs-10 nt guide pairs correlated r = 0.64-0.96 across five cell lines.
4. **Data type**: Human immortalized cell lines only — **Jurkat** (T lymphocyte; primary model, all ChIP-seq), **A375** (melanoma), **K562**, **MV4-11** (AML), and **HEK293/HEK293FT**. (Note: the paper's own text mislabels K562 as "T-lymphocytes"; K562 is a CML erythroleukemia line.) No primary cells, no animal model, no tissue.

**Relevance flag**: Directly relevant to both terms of the CellGuide metric. For **specificity**, it is one of the cleanest quantitative off-target datasets available for a dCas9 modality: 78/29,908 mismatched sites significantly perturbed, with an explicit positional rule (PAM-proximal/seed mismatches intolerable, 5'-distal and A/T-rich positions tolerable) that can be encoded as a mismatch-position weighting in an off-target penalty — and it shows binding-based (dCas9) targeting is far more mismatch-permissive than cleavage, so a Cas9-KO specificity model cannot be reused unchanged for CRISPRi. For **chromatin context**, only ~46.6% of sequence-match CTCF sites are actually CTCF-bound in Jurkat, and the paper explicitly recommends "calculating the fraction of sequence match sites bound by the target TF (via **ATAC-seq, ChIP-seq, or CUT&Tag data**)" to predict screen efficiency — i.e. it endorses using accessibility/occupancy tracks as a per-site efficacy prior, and demonstrates cell-type-specific outcomes (sg22 depleted in A375 only; sg2/sg23 in a subset of lines) despite largely transferable fitness effects across five lines. It also supplies a mechanistic efficiency readout (H3K9me3 deposition magnitude) rather than just phenotype.
