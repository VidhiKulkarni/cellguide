# Agent 2 structured extraction — srikanth2026_crispri_library_design

1. **Experimental design**: **CRISPRi** — catalytically dead SpCas9 fused to KRAB repressor domains, lentivirally delivered, assayed by pooled negative-selection viability screens and promoter-tiling screens; results feed a new on-target model (Rule Set 3 Interference, RS3i) and the genome-wide library **Katsano**. Systems compared: dCas9-Kox1, dCas9-Zim3, Kox1-MeCP2, N- versus C-terminal KRAB fusions, and ALFA-nanobody recruitment (5xALFA-dCas9 plus Zim3-Nb-ALFA), assembled with the Fragmid modular vector kit.
2. **Target genes**: Primary tiling library — 108,574 sgRNAs tiling 1,000 bp upstream and downstream of the MANE Select TSS of 201 essential and 198 nonessential genes; secondary library — a 30 essential / 30 nonessential gene subset. Katsano is genome-wide: 62,404 targeting sgRNAs over 20,106 genes (3 guides per TSS; 1,503 genes get distinct guide sets for multiple TSSs) plus 900 intergenic and 100 non-targeting controls. Specifically named genes: *CD47* and *CD55* (nanobody knockdown validation), *ZNF131* (alternative-TSS case study), *BRAF*, *MAP2K1*, *SOX10* (A375-selective dependencies), *BCR-ABL* and *GATA1* (K562-selective), and *CHEK1* and *SAP18* (essential hits recovered only via an alternative TSS).
3. **Outcomes**: On-target — RS3i is an XGBoost model built from just three features after SHAP-based selection: distance from the MANE Select TSS, RS3 Sequence score, and ATAC-seq peak overlap. Guides 0-75 bp downstream of the TSS are most active. 81.6% of guides with RS3i > 1.2 had signed z-score > 2 versus only 14.7% of guides with RS3i < 0 (Dolcetto replication: 80.4% versus 7.5%). Arrayed validation: Zim3-dCas9 knocked down CD47/CD55 by 93%/89%, the best nanobody configuration by 93%/74%; N-terminal KRAB fusions consistently outperform C-terminal; Kox1-MeCP2 gave no advantage over Kox1 alone. In A375 viability screens Katsano missed only 63 essential genes at FDR 5% versus 95 missed by Dolcetto, using half the guides per target, and performed comparably to the CRISPRko library Jacquere. Off-target — quantified via a new CRISPRi-specific **Seed Score**. CRISPick Aggregate CFD (the CRISPRko off-target metric) is a poor classifier of promiscuous CRISPRi guides. Instead, occurrence of "GG" (the canonical PAM dinucleotide) within the 12 bp PAM-proximal seed strongly predicts promiscuity: guides with Seed Score >= 3 capture 52.7% of promiscuous guides while comprising only 9% of nonpromiscuous guides, and also show significantly lower on-target activity. Non-canonical PAM dinucleotides AG and GA show the same trend more weakly. Katsano keeps under 1% of guides at Seed Score >= 3 versus 13.8% (Dolcetto) and 13.2% (hCRISPRiv2). Separately, about 17% of genes (8% using MANE Select only) have a TSS within 1 kb of another gene's TSS, a structural false-positive source; alternative-TSS usage causes a 1-2.5% false negative rate.
4. **Data type**: Human cancer cell lines only — A549 (lung) and HCT116 (colorectal) for the primary tiling screens, HCT116 for secondary tiling and nanobody tests, A375 (melanoma) and K562 (erythroleukemia) for the Katsano genome-wide validation screens. Public chromatin data drawn from ENCODE for A549, HCT116, K562, Panc1, MCF7, GM12878 and HepG2. Curated external tiling data from Nunez et al. (viability) and Gilbert et al. (ricin susceptibility). No primary cells or animal models.

**Relevance flag**: Highly relevant and close to a working template for CellGuide's efficiency term — a guide-scoring model that explicitly includes chromatin accessibility as a scored feature, with ATAC-seq peak overlap among the top three SHAP features alongside TSS offset and sequence score. Usable empirical detail: ATAC-seq and H3K4me3/H3K27ac ChIP-seq peaks discriminate active from inactive guides for most genes whereas DNase-seq (DHS) does not (about half of guides inside ATAC/ChIP peaks are missed by DHS), and accessibility is encoded as a binned fraction of cell lines in which the guide falls inside an ATAC peak. Its cross-cell-type transferability result is a caution for CellGuide's central hypothesis: the accessibility effect was consistent across all cell lines tested (so the authors treat it as generalizable, "although this will vary by gene"), and removing ATAC only mildly degraded RS3i — i.e. for CRISPRi at promoters, accessibility behaves more like a constitutive-promoter feature than a cell-context discriminator. The Seed Score additionally provides a modality-specific specificity term and shows that a CRISPRko CFD-based off-target penalty does not transfer to dCas9 systems.
