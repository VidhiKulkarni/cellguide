# Agent 2 structured extraction — xiang2025_glide_crispri_design

1. **Experimental design**: Computational — a web-based genome-scale CRISPRi sgRNA library design tool (GLiDe, https://www.thu-big.net/sgRNA_design/) for prokaryotes, plus a small wet-lab validation. The pipeline takes a FASTA plus GFF or PTT/RNT annotation, clusters paralogous genes with BLASTN (e-value < 0.001, >95% identity and >95% coverage) so multi-copy genes are not counted as off-targets, enumerates N20-NGG and N20-NAG candidate guides split by template/non-template strand, aligns their 12 bp PAM-proximal seed regions with SeqMap, applies off-target quality control, then filters on GC content. Validation used a two-plasmid dCas9 (pdCas9-J23111) plus sgRNA/mCherry reporter system in E. coli read out by flow cytometry (LSRFortessa), fitting log10 fluorescence to a two-component Gaussian mixture.
2. **Target genes**: Genome-scale, not a specific gene set — a built-in database of 1,397 prokaryotic species from NCBI RefSeq release 225. The illustrative library was designed for Corynebacterium glutamicum ATCC 13032; four specific guide/off-target gene pairs were experimentally tested (R1: a Cgl0025 guide hitting Cgl2364; R2: Cgl0044 hitting Cgl1968; R3: Cgl0005 hitting Cgl0369; R4: Cgl0059 hitting Cgl1680). Table S1 lists multi-copy gene clusters for E. coli MG1655.
3. **Outcomes**: On-target — explicitly out of scope: the paper states GLiDe "does not consider the on-target activity of sgRNAs", relying instead on ordering guides by proximity to the start codon/TSS and on including multiple guides per gene; the only on-target-adjacent heuristics are default GC limits of 30-85% (very high or very low GC guides are reported less active), a default 20-nt spacer, and a non-template-strand preference. Off-target — the paper's quantitative core. Two rules: (i) a seed-region rule, where off-target binding is not called if there are more than two mismatches in the 12 bp PAM-proximal region; and (ii) a penalty-scoring rule dividing the guide 3-prime to 5-prime into region I (7 nt), region II (5 nt) and region III (remainder), with per-mismatch penalties of 8 / 4.5 / 2.5 for NGG PAMs and 10 / 7 / 3 for NAG PAMs; a guide is discarded when the summed penalty x mismatch score for a candidate site falls below a user threshold (default 20, recommended range 16-21). Experimentally, four highly-ranked guides that CHOPCHOP and Synthego passed but GLiDe flagged all showed real off-target binding, repressing the mCherry reporter by 51.12% (R2) to 90.70% (R4), mean 69.47%, P < 1.36e-5 by two-tailed t-test, with blank controls confirming the effect was binding-driven. Negative-control guides are generated as random N20s with penalty score 25, matching GC limits, and no runs of five or more identical bases. Stated limitations: the penalty scores are empirical rather than thermodynamic and treat all mismatch nucleotide pairs equally, even though rU/dG, rG/dT and rG/dG are nearly as stable as Watson-Crick pairs.
4. **Data type**: Bacteria only — Escherichia coli MCm strain for all reporter experiments, E. coli MG1655 and Corynebacterium glutamicum ATCC 13032 genomes for library design, and a 1,397-species prokaryotic reference database. No mammalian cells, no primary cells, no animal model.

**Relevance flag**: Relevant as a transferable specificity-term formulation, not as biology. The positionally-weighted mismatch penalty (region I/II/III = 8/4.5/2.5 for NGG and 10/7/3 for NAG, summed and thresholded around 20) plus the "more than two seed-region mismatches means no binding" rule is a directly reusable, experimentally calibrated off-target scoring scheme for the specificity component, and the paper quantifies the cost of ignoring it (69% mean unintended repression from guides other tools ranked highly). Two caveats for this project: it models dCas9 binding (CRISPRi) rather than Cas9 cleavage, and it is prokaryotic — the authors explicitly note prokaryotic genomes differ in accessibility due to their less organized chromosome structure — so it offers nothing on chromatin accessibility, ATAC signal, or cross-cell-type transferability, and deliberately omits any on-target efficiency model.
